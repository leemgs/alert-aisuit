\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Guarantees.} Proposition~\ref{prop:termination} is a bounded termination-and-escalation property, not a convergence theorem. Its termination clause is close to definitional, and its progress assumption is not verified directly. Calibration controls evidence omission under its calibration distribution (150 cases), not correct legal conclusions, temporal-shift robustness, or freedom from LLM hallucinations~\citep{dahl2024hallucinating}. Because litigation streams are non-stationary, the omission guarantee is weakest under the temporal split (Table~\ref{tab:risk_control_temporal})---precisely where deployment needs it most---so we treat rolling or weighted re-calibration as a deployment requirement rather than an optional refinement.

\textbf{Generality is untested.} The validity-gating operator is defined generally, but all evidence comes from ALERT's own pipeline on U.S.\ AI-litigation data. Its transfer to \emph{external} retrieval stacks and corpora is specified as a protocol (Protocol~P1, Appendix~\ref{app:p1}) that has not been executed in full. A pilot on overruled U.S.\ Supreme Court precedent with gold closures did not meet its pre-fixed criterion: the gate removed stale cases on all three host retrievers but improved nDCG@10 significantly on only one. The gains reported here should therefore not be read as evidence that the gate improves other retrievers or other domains. Likewise, backbone robustness is limited: the open-weights re-run (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}) covers only the full ALERT system, not the baselines, so we cannot yet claim that ALERT's margin over baselines holds independently of the LLM backbone. Cross-stack transfer and backbone-matched baseline comparisons therefore remain the main open generality tests.

\textbf{Benchmark scale and evaluation.} At the current scale PLRE remains an initial benchmark over five product archetypes and 312 product-mapping cases. We do not report confidence intervals for Table~\ref{tab:plre_external} or for the intermediate step of Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ablation}, or results on a subset without stale-authority distractors (19.7\% of pairs). PLRE tables are computed over all 1{,}856 pairs, including those used to set the validation threshold, rather than a held-out slice. Since PLRE labels encode temporal validity, part of the gating gain may come from rejecting such distractors rather than from better entailment. PLRE results should therefore be read as directional evidence for the validity-gated formulation rather than as precise effect sizes or coverage of all AI-product litigation. The report-quality study (RQ2) uses two blinded raters on 15 cases (mean $\kappa=0.68$) without multiple-comparison correction, and is treated as supporting, not primary, evidence. Because 847 of 1{,}247 labels are retained pre-labels, all primary significance claims are restricted to the human-adjudicated test split of 82 cases, for which Table~\ref{tab:human_only_main} reports point estimates without confidence intervals, and effect sizes over the strongest baseline are modest (Table~\ref{tab:human_only_main}).

\textbf{Coverage and jurisdiction.} Cases are counted per docket, so disputes litigated across several related or consolidated dockets are over-represented relative to distinct disputes (App.~\ref{app:inclusion}). The dataset is also skewed toward the largest defendants---the top three account for 41.3\% of cases---limiting representativeness for smaller vendors. ALERT targets U.S.\ federal CourtListener/RECAP coverage and English-language documents; non-U.S.\ jurisdictions require different docket schemas and citation practices, and extending the treatment graph to them is future work.

\textbf{Extractor recall.} Negative-treatment edge recall (0.77 internal gold; 20.4\% missed-action rate against an authoritative citator, counting missed closures and missed down-weights) is useful but not lossless, and a missed action is exactly what allows a stale precedent to leak into a risk decision; ALERT mitigates this with human-review routing for low-confidence closures, but improving extraction recall remains an open problem.
