
\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We address six research questions: triage-label agreement (RQ1), report quality (RQ2), loop and temporal-graph ablations (RQ3), schema completeness (RQ4), PLRE (RQ5), and evidence-coverage risk control (RQ6).

\textbf{Dataset split and label provenance.} We partition ALERT-Dataset into stratified 70/10/20 train/validation/test splits by year and litigation type. Because 847 of 1{,}247 labels originate from an automated pre-labeler and 400 are human-adjudicated, primary significance claims are computed only on the human-adjudicated subset; retained-label results are reported only for scale. This keeps the rule-based pre-labeler out of the evidence for our primary claims.

\textbf{Baselines.} We compare ALERT with five risk-classification baselines: \textbf{B1 Rule-Based} (the ingestion-layer scorer); \textbf{B2 Single-Pass LLM}; \textbf{B3 RAG-only}; \textbf{B4 Single-Agent} (ALERT without the goal-conditioned loop); and \textbf{B5 Compute-Matched Self-Refinement} (a Reflexion-style baseline~\citep{shinn2023reflexion} given ALERT's mean budget of 2.4 iterations). For PLRE we add non-ALERT baselines that isolate the suspected confounds: product--theory cosine, LegalBERT product--theory NLI, COLIEE-style~\citep{rabelo2022coliee} precedent retrieval with no interval closure, and ALERT without temporal gating. These baselines separate product--theory matching, legal entailment, retrieval quality, test-time compute, and the hard validity gate.

\textbf{Evaluation metrics.} We report macro F1/precision/recall over three severity labels, report-quality ratings by two blinded specialists, loop efficiency, schema completeness, PLRE P/R/F1, edge-error propagation, and evidence-coverage omission risk under calibrated thresholds. Severity-label F1 measures triage-label agreement, not legal liability. Significance uses a paired bootstrap over test predictions ($10{,}000$ resamples); calibrated omission risk and backbone robustness are reported in the appendix, with hyperparameters in Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams}.

\subsection{RQ1: Triage-Label Agreement}

Table~\ref{tab:human_only_main} reports severity-label agreement on the human-adjudicated subset, our primary circularity check. ALERT outperforms the compute-matched self-refinement baseline (B5) by 4 F1 points (paired bootstrap, $p<0.05$). The marginal intervals overlap, but the paired bootstrap scores per-item differences on shared test cases and thus removes the between-case variance that dominates them; we nonetheless read the gap over B5 as modest. On the full held-out test set (including retained pre-labels; last column and Table~\ref{tab:rq1}) the same ordering holds.

\subsection{RQ2: Report Quality}

Two blinded legal specialists rated the reports of ALERT and B2--B5 for the same 15 cases (75 reports) on 5-point Accuracy, Completeness, Actionability, Evidence Sufficiency, and Clarity scales. ALERT scores 4.3/4.1/4.2/4.4/3.9; case-paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests give $p<0.05$ against every baseline, with the largest gain in Evidence Sufficiency. With 15 pairs per test, no correction for the 20 comparisons, and no inter-rater agreement, RQ2 is supporting evidence only.

\subsection{RQ3: Agentic Loop and Temporal Graph Ablations}

Because temporal precedent reasoning is the central claim, Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ablation} decomposes validity-interval gating and treatment-edge weighting. Removing treatment weighting and then interval gating each degrades PLRE F1. Only the full-vs-flat gap of 6 F1 points was tested for significance ($p<0.01$); the intermediate 3-point step is reported without an interval and should be read as directional. Stale citations rise from 4.1\% to 12.7\%. The failure mode is exactly what Eq.~\eqref{eq:tpg_score} targets: flat retrieval can rank a lexically close but already-closed authority above a still-operative one, changing which evidence reaches the generator rather than merely adding a warning after generation. This is the paper's main methodological result.

Table~\ref{tab:edge_sensitivity} measures how closure errors propagate. The extractor row already operates at the observed miss rate (about 23\% of gold closures, implied by 0.77 recall) and costs 2 PLRE F1 points relative to gold edges. Rows below additionally mask a random fraction of the \emph{extracted} closures before retrieval, simulating a weaker extractor: removing a further 23\% of extracted closures ($\approx$41\% effective miss rate) roughly doubles the stale-cite rate (4.1\%$\rightarrow$8.9\%) and lowers PLRE F1 by 4 points. ALERT therefore treats low-confidence negative edges as self-verification failures: they are blocked from automatic interval closure, trigger retrieval of independent treatment evidence, and are surfaced as unresolved legal-status atoms if the Goal Checker cannot satisfy coverage. This critique loop is what motivates the agentic structure: it localizes extractor uncertainty before it becomes an unqualified PLRE support claim.

Component ablations for the agentic loop confirm each part contributes: removing the Goal Checker, Planner, and Retriever query-expansion lowers risk-classification F1 from 0.81 to 0.76/0.78/0.79 respectively, and only query-expansion removal degrades retrieval (Recall@10 $0.84\!\rightarrow\!0.76$); details are in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

\subsection{RQ4: Sensing Schema Completeness}

As an operational check (not evidence of legal correctness), automated records for 150 newly detected cases reach 97.2\% required-field completeness and cut record-creation time from 18.1 to 1.4 minutes (Table~\ref{tab:schema_completeness}, appendix).

\subsection{RQ5: Product--Litigation Risk Entailment}

PLRE is evaluated on anonymized AI product specs spanning five archetypes and 312 product-mapping cases, so it is an initial benchmark. We report its scale at the case level; pair-level statistics (number of attribute--theory--date pairs, positive rate, share of stale-authority distractors) and a PLRE-specific inter-annotator agreement are not reported (the $\kappa$ in \S\ref{sec:dataset} is for severity labels). Positive labels require a product attribute, a legal-risk theory, a query date, and time-valid supporting authority; negative labels include ordinary mismatches and stale-authority distractors. In Table~\ref{tab:plre_external}, ALERT reaches 0.78 F1, 5--13 points above the external baselines; removing temporal gating costs 6 points (decomposed in Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ablation}). Because these comparisons carry no confidence intervals and the benchmark is small, we read them as directional evidence for the validity-gated formulation, not as coverage of all AI-product litigation.

\subsection{RQ6: Evidence-Coverage Risk Control and Temporal Shift}

Calibrating the evidence threshold to $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ yields omission risk 0.043/0.082/0.171 in random splits. Under a temporal split (2023--24 $\rightarrow$ 2025) the achieved risk rises to 0.061/0.113/0.196, exceeding the target at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and $0.10$ (Table~\ref{tab:risk_control_temporal}), which motivates rolling re-calibration. To probe dependence on GPT-4o, we re-run the full system with an open-weights Llama-3.1-70B+bge stack: it runs unchanged, with macro F1 dropping from 0.81 to 0.76 and similar loop length (2.4 vs.\ 2.6 iterations; Table~\ref{tab:robustness}). We did not re-run the baselines on this stack, so this shows only that the pipeline transfers to an open-weights backbone, not that its advantage over baselines is backbone-independent.
