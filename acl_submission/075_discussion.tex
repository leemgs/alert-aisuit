\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What drives the gains.} ALERT's reusable contribution is the coupling of a point-in-time validity gate (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tpg_score}) with coverage-controlled evidence verification---not citators, agents, or the ingestion schema. Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ablation} isolates the gate, and the compute-matched B5 baseline shows the gain is not explained by extra LLM calls alone. The operator is general in form; we instantiate and evaluate it for law.

\textbf{Construct validity.} Two validity checks are separated. Agreement of the treatment graph with an authoritative citator (Protocol~P2, Appendix~\ref{app:p2}) is measured: negative-treatment micro-F1 0.81, a 20.4\% missed-closure rate, and a 3.8\% PLRE decision-flip rate after citator correction. Transfer of the gate to external retrieval stacks (Protocol~P1, Appendix~\ref{app:p1}) is pre-registered but not yet executed and remains the main open generality test.

\textbf{Error propagation.} The edge extractor is not trusted as an oracle: a negative-treatment edge must pass citation-span anchoring, date consistency, and confidence checks before it can close an interval; otherwise it becomes an unresolved goal atom that forces targeted retrieval or human escalation. The agentic loop thus serves as a mechanism for localizing graph uncertainty.
