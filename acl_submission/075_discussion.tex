\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What drives the gains.} ALERT's reusable contribution is the coupling of a point-in-time validity gate (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tpg_score}) with coverage-controlled evidence verification---not citators, agents, or the ingestion schema. Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ablation} isolates the gate, and the compute-matched B5 baseline shows the gain is not explained by extra LLM calls alone. The operator is general in form, but our evidence is confined to one domain (U.S.\ AI litigation) and one system; we do not yet claim that its gains transfer.

\textbf{Construct validity.} Two validity checks are separated. Agreement of the treatment graph with an authoritative citator (Protocol~P2, Appendix~\ref{app:p2}) is measured: negative-treatment micro-F1 0.81, a 20.4\% missed-closure rate, and a 3.8\% PLRE decision-flip rate after citator correction. Transfer of the gate to external retrieval stacks is specified as Protocol~P1 (Appendix~\ref{app:p1}), with conditions, metrics, and acceptance criteria fixed in advance, but has not yet been executed; it is the main open generality test.

\textbf{Error propagation.} The edge extractor is not trusted as an oracle: a negative-treatment edge must pass citation-span anchoring, date consistency, and confidence checks before it can close an interval; otherwise it becomes an unresolved goal atom that forces targeted retrieval or human escalation. The agentic loop thus serves as a mechanism for localizing graph uncertainty.
