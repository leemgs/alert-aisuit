\section{Introduction}

Generative-AI products increasingly face litigation over training data, output reproduction, model behavior, and downstream misuse. For the engineering organizations that build them, the operational question is not merely whether a lawsuit exists, but whether a theory of liability maps to a specific product attribute, whether the supporting authority was valid at the relevant date, and which mitigation should be reviewed by counsel. Keyword alerts surface noisy filings, docket trackers rarely map cases to product features, and conventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\citep{lewis2020retrieval} treats semantically similar legal documents as comparable even when later decisions have narrowed or superseded them.

We formalize this need as \emph{Product--Litigation Risk Entailment} (PLRE): deciding whether product attributes entail exposure to a legal-risk theory under time-valid precedent. PLRE differs from standard legal textual entailment~\citep{rabelo2022coliee} in three ways: the premise is an engineering description rather than legal text, the relation is directional (product attribute $\rightarrow$ risk theory), and the support set is conditioned on which authority was operative at the query date. Legal citators such as Shepard's and KeyCite already encode the intuition that authority changes over time; we instead build a public-corpus, automatically extracted, time-indexed treatment layer and connect it to product-facing entailment rather than retrospective case lookup.

We present ALERT (\textbf{Agentic Legal Exposure and Risk Tracking}), a continuous sensing system built around this task. Its core mechanism is a point-in-time retrieval operator (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tpg_score}) that changes the candidate evidence distribution \emph{before} generation, rather than flagging stale citations after an LLM has already produced a report. This matters because stale but lexically similar precedent can dominate ordinary RAG. Around this operator, a goal-conditioned agentic loop exposes unmet evidence requirements and stops when calibrated evidence coverage is satisfied. The operator is not legal-specific: it applies to any time-stamped corpus in which later documents can supersede earlier ones.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item a supersession-aware temporal retrieval mechanism that turns validity intervals and treatment edges into a retrieval-time authority gate (\S\ref{sec:tpg});
  \item PLRE, an initial product-conditioned legal-risk entailment benchmark with non-ALERT baselines and ablations that separate temporal validity from flat similarity (\S\ref{sec:plre}, \S\ref{sec:evaluation});
  \item a goal-conditioned agentic loop whose stopping rule controls evidence coverage, with a conformal omission-risk guarantee (\S\ref{sec:goal_checker});
  \item ALERT-Dataset, with per-record label provenance, annotation guidelines, and a human-adjudicated evaluation subset (\S\ref{sec:dataset}), plus a 30-day live deployment.
\end{itemize}
