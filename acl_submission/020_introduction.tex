\section{Introduction}

Generative-AI products increasingly face litigation over training data, output reproduction, model behavior, and downstream misuse. For the engineering organizations that build them, the operational question is not merely whether a lawsuit exists, but whether a theory of liability maps to a specific product attribute, whether the supporting authority was valid at the relevant date, and which mitigation should be reviewed by counsel. Keyword alerts surface noisy filings, docket trackers rarely map cases to product features, and conventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\citep{lewis2020retrieval} treats semantically similar legal documents as comparable even when later decisions have narrowed or superseded them.

We formalize this need as \emph{Product--Litigation Risk Entailment} (PLRE): deciding whether product attributes entail exposure to a legal-risk theory under time-valid precedent. PLRE differs from standard legal textual entailment~\citep{rabelo2022coliee} in three ways: the premise is an engineering description rather than legal text, the relation is directional (product attribute $\rightarrow$ risk theory), and the support set is conditioned on which authority was operative at the query date. Unlike commercial citators such as Shepard's and KeyCite, we build a public-corpus, automatically extracted, time-indexed treatment layer and connect it to product-facing entailment.

We present ALERT (\textbf{Agentic Legal Exposure and Risk Tracking}), a continuous sensing system built around this task. Its core component is a supersession-aware treatment layer: it infers when each authority stops being operative from treatment edges in \emph{later} opinions, and a simple gate (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tpg_score}) applies this before generation, rather than flagging stale citations after an LLM has already produced a report. Around this layer, a goal-conditioned agentic loop exposes unmet evidence requirements and stops when calibrated evidence coverage is satisfied. The layer's form is not specific to law---it is defined for any time-stamped corpus in which later documents can supersede earlier ones---but we evaluate it only for law, within ALERT.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item a supersession-aware treatment layer: treatment edges extracted from public opinions, validated against gold spans and a commercial citator, and propagated into validity intervals that gate retrieval at query time (\S\ref{sec:tpg});
  \item PLRE, an initial product-conditioned legal-risk entailment benchmark with non-ALERT baselines and ablations that separate temporal validity from flat similarity (\S\ref{sec:plre}, \S\ref{sec:evaluation});
  \item a goal-conditioned agentic loop whose stopping rule controls evidence coverage, with a conformal omission-risk guarantee (\S\ref{sec:goal_checker});
  \item ALERT-Dataset, with per-record label provenance, annotation guidelines, and a human-adjudicated evaluation subset (\S\ref{sec:dataset}), plus a 30-day live deployment (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}), and a public, licence-free testbed for supersession-aware retrieval built from overruled U.S.\ Supreme Court precedent (Appendix~\ref{app:p1}).
\end{itemize}
